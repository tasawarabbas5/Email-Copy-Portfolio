# Copywriting formulas and frameworks applied to email

Scope note: ~12 tool calls; WebFetch failed (DNS) for Wikipedia and Mailchimp, so everything below rests on WebSearch result summaries, not full-page reads. Many top results were vendor blogs or AI "skill" aggregator pages; these are flagged. Items I know from background reading but could not source are listed under Gaps as "unsourced background, verify before publishing".

## What are the exact steps of each framework, and which email type does each suit best?

### Takeaway
Most email formulas are 3-6 step sequences that move from attention to problem/desire to proof to a single ask. Sources agree on the basic steps of AIDA, PAS, BAB, 4Ps, 4Us, QUEST, PASTOR, Hook-Story-Offer and Star-Story-Solution; origins are well documented only for AIDA (partly), the 4Us and PASTOR. Several others (Seinfeld emails, Soap Opera Sequence) are contested between practitioners.

### Cited Findings
- AIDA = Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Desire is built with social proof, scarcity, exclusivity or showing life change; one guide says it suits shorter emails and landing pages — [Mailchimp (IT page, via search)](https://mailchimp.com/it/resources/copwriting-formulas/); [Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/aida-copywriting) (its conversion statistics are unverified; ignore them).
- AIDA origin: most sources credit E. St. Elmo Lewis (1872-1948), with the "attract attention, maintain interest, create desire" slogan dated about 1898 per Edward K. Strong's 1925 account, "get action" added later; the acronym AIDA is attributed to C.P. Russell in Printers' Ink, 1921; sources disagree on dates and some say Lewis did not use "AIDA" himself, and one mentions E.W. Scripps (1880s) as possible earlier influence. Label: partly documented, partly folklore — [E. St. Elmo Lewis, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._St._Elmo_Lewis); [EBSCO AIDA overview](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/marketing/aida-model); [Martech Zone](https://martech.zone/acronym/aida/).
- PAS = Problem, Agitate, Solve. Problem in subject/headline, first paragraphs agitate, then solution. Recommended for direct-response channels such as cold email and ads, and for audiences already looking for a solution — [MailBakery](https://mailbakery.com/blog/best-copywriting-formulas-for-email-marketing/); [Yesware](https://www.yesware.com/blog/email-copywriting/). ("Problem-Agitate-Solve" is the same formula; no separate steps found.) Origin not established in my sources.
- BAB = Before (current problem), After (solved state), Bridge (how to get there). Suits case studies and customer transformation stories — [MailBakery](https://mailbakery.com/blog/best-copywriting-formulas-for-email-marketing/); [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/it/resources/copwriting-formulas/).
- Selection heuristic offered by one vendor: AIDA for immediate action, BAB for relationship building, PAS for problem-aware audiences — [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/it/resources/copwriting-formulas/). Practitioner heuristic only, not tested.
- PASTOR is credited to Ray Edwards, who says he uses it for every piece of sales copy; the name plays on "pastor" = shepherd. Edwards frames the long length of sales copy as needed to answer objections up front. Letter labels vary by source: Person/Problem/Pain, Amplify (aspirations/Amplify), Story/Solution/Testimony, Transformation, Offer, Response; derivative versions differ from Edwards's own wording — [Ray Edwards Show](https://rayedwards.libsyn.com/why-your-copy-fails-pastor-review); [Karrierebibel](https://karrierebibel.de/pastor-formel/); [thinkinsights](https://thinkinsights.net/index.php/consulting/pastor-framework). Attribution to Edwards: well-documented (his own podcast).
- 4Ps (Picture, Promise, Prove, Push): Picture paints the dream/problem, Promise shows life after the product, Prove gives data/social proof/demo, Push gives a clear next step — [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/the-4-ps-of-persuasive-copywriting); [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/promise-picture-proof-push/). Note naming variance: some sources say "Promise, Picture, Prove, Push" (order differs). Origin not established.
- 4Us (Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful) is a checklist for headlines/subject lines, credited to Michael Masterson (Early to Rise), applied to email subject lines by Robert Bly; scoring 1-4 per U; Unbounce adapted to landing pages — [Bly](https://bly.com/Pages/DMNCOL1e[1].htm); [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/podcast/copywriting-formula-4-u/); [Do It! Marketing](https://hub.doitmarketing.com/blog/bid/78939/Marketing-Concept-Use-these-headline-techniques-if-you-dare). It is an evaluation tool, not a body structure.
- QUEST = Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition; suggested for email sequences, content, webinar scripts (single secondary source) — [Skillselion summary](https://skillselion.com/skills/thatrebeccarae/claude-marketing/copywriting-frameworks). Low-quality source; origin not established.
- Star-Story-Solution: first-person story with a relatable character, solution shown as natural outcome; more story-driven than BAB; popular in cold email — [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/star-story-solution-copywriting-formula-for-lead-generation); [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/star-story-solution/).
- Hook-Story-Offer: Hook draws reader in, Story shows a relatable change, Offer invites them to apply it; credited to Russell Brunson, Dotcom Secrets (not to Haddad/Kern — I could not confirm that link) — [Ship 30](https://ship30for30.com/post/the-hook-story-offer-framework-an-easy-copywriting-formula-for-beginners); [WP Funnels](https://getwpfunnels.com/hook-story-offer/).
- Soap Opera Sequence: multi-day series opening loops in one email and closing in others, building one storyline; one aggregator lists 5-7 emails (set stage, backstory/struggle, epiphany, benefits, CTA, future pacing). Attribution conflict: Lean Labs credits Brunson; Andre Chaperon (AutoResponder Madness) is the practitioner often linked to story sequences — [Lean Labs](https://www.leanlabs.com/blog/using-soap-opera-email-to-tell-your-story-and-convert-leads); [Everyone Hates Marketers (Chaperon)](https://everyonehatesmarketers.com/podcast/andre-chaperon); [SuperFast Business](https://www.superfastbusiness.com/tag/autoresponder/).
- Seinfeld emails: per a Dotcom Secrets reader highlight, sent after the Soap Opera Sequence to broadcast-list subscribers, tied to real-time events in the "Attractive Character's" life, humor and storytelling with little pitching — [Goodreads highlight](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/53504694-dotcom-secrets/123802880-rory-o-brien/5caf98c0-0421-4c11-bda3-c2b1e2d081c1). Brunson attribution is single-source; no evidence found linking it to Chaperon.
- Open loops / Zeigarnik effect: marketers end emails before the payoff to drive the next open; psychological basis dates to 1920s studies with mixed replication; no controlled email A/B tests found — [Vero](https://www.getvero.com/resources/zeigarnik-effect/); [Atticus Li](https://www.atticusli.com/blog/posts/zeigarnik-effect-email-marketing-incomplete-tasks-drive-opens/); [Sequencio "Slippery Slide"](https://sequencio.substack.com/p/the-slippery-slide-sequence).
- Rule of One (one email, one job, one CTA; extended to one idea per paragraph, tied to cognitive load): repeating the same CTA link is fine — [500.co](https://500.co/blog/one-email-one-cta); [InBoXer](https://inbxr.beehiiv.com/p/one-email-one-job-this-rule-skyrockets-your-click-through-rates-db2c). Origin of the name "Rule of One" not established; vendor heuristics, not tested.
- Ben Settle: daily emails meant to entertain, educate, elevate, building relationship before pitching; Email Players is his paid print newsletter on email copywriting; summaries of "Email Players" contents were low-quality and unverified — [Robert Plank podcast summary](https://www.robertplank.com/334-daily-email-marketing-ben-settle/); [Copywriter Club](https://thecopywriterclub.com/ben-settle/); [Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/?p=46032).
- Cialdini's six principles (reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity); Influence first published 1984; email application = give first (free resource), scarcity via deadlines/limited seats; practitioner guidance, not measured email results — [Vero](https://www.getvero.com/resources/weapons-of-persuasion/); [Movable Ink](https://movableink.com/blog/how-to-leverage-the-6-principles-of-persuasion-in-your-email-marketing); [Zettasphere](https://www.zettasphere.com/persuasion-psychology-in-email/).
- Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers conversion copywriting: start from research; choose messaging to optimize, write questions, collect answers from customers/prospects, tag repeated words and phrases line by line, answer in customer's own language; "message mining" of reviews and comments (Amazon, TripAdvisor) alongside surveys and interviews — [Wistia interview](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/joanna-wiebe-conversion-copywriting).
- Halbert: core lesson "starving crowd" (market beats clever copy); Boron Letters written to his son from prison in Boron, CA; "A-pile" test (opened? read? what's missing?). Sources are third-party summaries — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/gary-halbert-business-lessons/); [BeFreed](https://www.befreed.ai/book/the-boron-letters-by-gary-c-halbert).

### Inferences
- Structurally, AIDA, 4Ps and PASTOR are the same arc (hook, desire/problem, proof, ask) at different granularity; PAS/BAB are compressed versions for short emails; Star-Story-Solution/Hook-Story-Offer are story-first variants suited to nurture/cold email; Soap Opera/Seinfeld operate at sequence level rather than single email.
- Suggested fit: PAS/AIDA/4Ps for promo and cold emails; BAB for case-study emails; PASTOR for long launch/sales emails; QUEST/Soap Opera for nurture sequences; Seinfeld/Settle-style for ongoing broadcasts; 4Us for subject lines.

### Gaps
- No sourced results for: FAB, APP (Agree-Promise-Preview or Atteneuer? ambiguous), ACCA, the "1-2-3-4 formula", Dan Kennedy, Clayton Makepeace, John Carlton, Jobs-to-be-Done, Hero's Journey in email. Not reported rather than guessed.
- Unsourced background (training knowledge, verify): FAB = Features, Advantages, Benefits (sales-training origin); ACCA = Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action (often attributed to Dr. Ernest Dichter / Colley's DAGMAR, folklore-grade); APP usually = Agree, Promise, Preview (Brian Clark / Copyblogger) or Admit-Promise-Prove; Kennedy "No B.S." principles, Makepeace "greatest fear/desire" emotional core, Carlton "Simple Writing System" — unverified here.
- Primary texts (Breakthrough Advertising, Cashvertising, Boron Letters, Settle's Email Players) not read.

## How do awareness stages (Schwartz) change the email angle?

### Takeaway
Schwartz's stages (Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware) dictate the lead: unaware readers need a problem/story hook, most-aware readers need only the offer. Market sophistication is a separate five-stage model and sources conflate and disagree on its labels.

### Cited Findings
- Stages: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware (needs little but the right offer); sequences can nurture from cold to ready-to-buy; one broadcast message across all stages fails at most of them — [Selzee](https://selzee.com/eugene-schwartz-5-levels-of-awareness); [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/five-stages-awareness-lead-generation/); [Rainmaker.fm](https://rainmaker.fm/?p=161672).
- Publication date conflict: most sources say Breakthrough Advertising 1966; one says 1952 (likely error) — [Adbeat](https://blog.adbeat.com/?p=804).
- Market sophistication per one summary: stage 1 state the core claim simply; 2 enlarge/claim superiority; 3 introduce a unique mechanism; 4 elaborate mechanism or answer cynicism; 5 identification with the prospect. Sources differ in labels; verify against the book — [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/5-stages-of-market-sophistication/); [ones.com](https://ones.com/blog/?p=1145980); [BeFreed](https://www.befreed.ai/ja/book/breakthrough-advertising-by-eugene-m-schwartz).

### Inferences
- Email mapping: welcome/cold-lead sequences for unaware/problem-aware = story or PAS leads; solution-aware = comparison/mechanism; product-aware = proof, objections; most-aware (cart abandon, flash sale) = short offer-first emails.

### Gaps
- No email-specific data on stage-targeted performance.

## How do practitioners adapt formulas to short vs long emails?

### Takeaway
Sources suggest AIDA/PAS/BAB for shorter emails and PASTOR-length for long sales copy because objections must be pre-answered; the Rule of One compresses any formula to one idea and one CTA.

### Cited Findings
- AIDA recommended for shorter emails — [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/it/resources/copwriting-formulas/). Edwards: long copy exists to answer objections ahead of time — [Ray Edwards Show](https://rayedwards.libsyn.com/why-your-copy-fails-pastor-review). Single CTA, repeated link ok — [500.co](https://500.co/blog/one-email-one-cta). Settle-style daily emails prioritize entertainment/relationship over selling each time — [Robert Plank](https://www.robertplank.com/334-daily-email-marketing-ben-settle/).

### Inferences
- Long emails can be PASTOR/story; short emails drop to P-A or B-A-B with one link.

### Gaps
- No tested length-vs-conversion data found.

## Where do the formulas fail or get overused?

### Takeaway
Evidence of failure is thin; criticisms are mostly structural: formulas lack audience research, sequences confuse late joiners, and psychological claims (Zeigarnik) are weakly replicated.

### Cited Findings
- Soap-opera sequences criticized because mid-sequence joiners lack backstory — [Everyone Hates Marketers](https://everyonehatesmarketers.com/podcast/andre-chaperon).
- Zeigarnik basis from small 1920s studies with mixed replication; no controlled email tests of open loops or single-CTA — [Atticus Li](https://www.atticusli.com/blog/posts/zeigarnik-effect-email-marketing-incomplete-tasks-drive-opens/) (search summary's own caveat).
- Vendor performance claims (e.g. Zeliq reply-rate figures) unverified — [Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/aida-copywriting).
- Wiebe's process puts customer language research ahead of formulas — [Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/joanna-wiebe-conversion-copywriting); Halbert: market first — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/gary-halbert-business-lessons/).
- Cialdini tactics (e.g., scarcity) warned to be used authentically — [Movable Ink](https://movableink.com/blog/how-to-leverage-the-6-principles-of-persuasion-in-your-email-marketing).

### Inferences
- Formula overuse leads to sameness (PAS agitation across every inbox) — inference, no source.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying formula fatigue or A/B comparisons between formulas.
