# Campaign, Broadcast and Outbound Email Types in Email Copywriting

Research date: 2026-10-11. Method: web searches (WebFetch of instantly.ai failed with a DNS error, so everything below comes from search-result summaries, not full-page reads). Many sources are vendor blogs or third-party summaries; this is flagged per item. Primary practitioner material (Chaperon, Settle, Walker, Kennedy books) was NOT reachable, so practitioner structures are thin and marked as gaps.

## 1. Cold outreach / sales prospecting emails and follow-ups (and how cold differs from warm)

### Takeaway
Cold email is short (roughly under 80 words), asks for interest rather than a calendar slot on first touch, and runs a 4-7 step sequence in which each follow-up adds a new angle. Benchmarks come from sending-tool vendors and are directional only.

### Cited Findings
- Instantly's 2026 benchmark (based on 2025 sending data) gives a platform-wide average reply rate of 3.43%, with top performers above 10%; its earlier 2025 report gave 1.6% average and 6-7% for top performers, so the two years are not directly comparable. Reply.io's study used a 3.02% baseline. — [Search summary of Instantly 2026 report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); [Folderly 2025 stats](https://generate.folderly.com/blog/cold-email-statistics-2025)
- Length: Instantly's best campaigns keep emails under 80 words; Reply.io research found 50-70 word emails get on average 9% more replies; a Hunter study (cited by FullEnrich) found 20-39 words had the highest average reply rate (4.5%), for simple asks. — [Instantly sequence guide](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-sequence-best-practices/); [Reply.io research PDF](https://reply.io/wp-content/uploads/How-to-write-better-cold-emails-Reply-Research.pdf); [FullEnrich](https://www.fullenrich.com/content/how-long-should-a-cold-email-be)
- Sequence: Instantly recommends 4-7 steps spaced 3-7 days apart; 58% of replies arrive on step 1 and 42% on later steps; follow-ups should add a new value or angle, never "just checking in". Unify suggests 3-5 follow-ups (4-6 touches) across 2-3 channels over about 2-3 weeks. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-sequence-best-practices/); [Unify](https://webflow.unifygtm.com/explore/cold-email-follow-up-strategy)
- Deliverability: bounce rate under 2% is cited as ideal (single vendor source). — [Folderly](https://generate.folderly.com/blog/cold-email-statistics-2025)
- CTA: Gong Labs analyzed 304,174 emails (meeting booked within 10 days). Three CTA types: specific time, open-ended meeting, and "interest" (is this worth exploring?). On cold first touches the interest CTA won; Gong's template guide says asking for interest is 2x as effective as asking for time. The specific ask performs better once interest exists (secondary source: 15% cold vs 37% after interest, truncated, verify against Gong). Gong piece originally May 2020, updated March 2026. — [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings/); [Gong templates PDF](https://www.gong.io/files/gong-template-cold-email-templates.pdf)
- Unverified third-party add-ons to the Gong data (42% reply lift, 44% drop for time requests, 10.7% top-sender reply rate) could not be confirmed in Gong's own material; do not use. — [growleads.io](https://growleads.io/blog/interest-based-ctas-vs-meeting-requests-study/); [prospeo.io](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-call-to-action)

### Inferences
- Cold anatomy that fits the data: one-line relevant opener (personalized to the prospect or trigger) -> one-sentence value/problem -> interest CTA question. Follow-ups: new proof point, new angle, then a short break-up email.
- Cold differs from warm broadcast in: no brand equity, so brevity and relevance carry everything; interest-CTA instead of hard CTA; deliverability hygiene matters as much as copy.

### Gaps
- No Close.com, Woodpecker, Apollo or Lemlist data retrieved; no personalization-depth study; no subject-line data (Gong search returned none). Reply benchmarks are vendor-sourced and self-selected.

## 2. Product launch sequences (Jeff Walker, cart-open/cart-close)

### Takeaway
Walker's formula is built from stories, sequences and triggers: a pre-launch content phase builds anticipation, a time-limited open-cart window creates scarcity, then the cart closes. Only secondary summaries were found.

### Cited Findings
- Components are stories, sequences and triggers; each message builds on the last so interest rises before the product is available. — [Summary of Launch](https://blog.voitto.com.br/resumo/en/launch)
- Pre-launch uses free content (videos, posts, webinars, Q&A) and can surface early objections; open cart is the official buying date; limited-time cart creates scarcity; post-launch continues delivering value to buyers. — [Voitto summary](https://blog.voitto.com.br/resumo/en/launch); [Smart Passive Income](https://smartpassiveincome.com/session507)
- A skills-directory page suggests a 5-7 day cart window with several emails on the final day, and claims 50% of sales come on the last day; that page gives no source, so treat the claim as unverified. — [claudeskills.info](https://claudeskills.info/skills/guia-matthieu/clawfu-skills/launch-formula/)

### Inferences
- Typical cart-close email logic (reasoned, not sourced): reminder of the offer, FAQ/objection email, testimonial/case study email, then deadline emails (e.g. 24 hours, hours-left, final hour) with a single CTA.

### Gaps
- No primary source for Walker's exact email-by-email timing, or launch email length/tone. No Brunson/other launch case data.

## 3. Story / "soap opera" sequences (Brunson, Chaperon) and daily email (Ben Settle)

### Takeaway
Brunson's Soap Opera Sequence is five emails warming new leads through serialized story with cliffhangers; Chaperon is known for serialized storytelling; Settle for high-frequency personality-led emails. Details of Chaperon and Settle's own methods were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Brunson: 5 emails to warm up new leads and build trust in you personally; email 1 sets the stage with backstory and a non-pushy CTA, hinting at a secret or transformation; open and close loops to pull readers between emails; email 5 gives a final push to act; Brunson reportedly then recommends daily emails. — [Lean Labs](https://www.leanlabs.com/blog/using-soap-opera-email-to-tell-your-story-and-convert-leads); [Mailmodo guide](https://mailmodo.com/guides/soap-opera-sequence); [Goodreads reader highlights](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/53504694-dotcom-secrets/176478893-jari-gruyaert/74075de6-4299-494d-a282-be73e80b7689)
- Third-party template of five beats (hook, backstory, obstacle, epiphany, resolution) with each email ending on an unresolved hook is someone else's adaptation, not Brunson or Chaperon. — [skillsmp.com](https://skillsmp.com/ar/creators/karausab590-ops/clawads-marketing-skills/soap-opera-sequence)
- Chaperon is described as a storytelling-focused email expert known for Autoresponder Madness; soap-opera sequences are a topic in his interview. Structure not explained. — [SuperFastBusiness](https://www.superfastbusiness.com/tag/autoresponder/)
- Settle: daily emails should entertain, educate and elevate, focus on audience problems, and draw from an idea file of personal experiences and observations; "Seinfeld email" style is emails about nothing in particular that live or die on being interesting. Claim that Brunson invented the Seinfeld label is weakly sourced and contested. — [Copywriter Club ep. 10](https://thecopywriterclub.com/ben-settle/); [Sequenzy](https://www.sequenzy.com/blog/seinfeld-email-sequence); [Mailrelay](https://mailrelay.com/en/?p=37364)

### Inferences
- Story emails differ from promos by delaying the pitch and using open loops as the reason to open the next email.

### Gaps
- Chaperon's actual method (tension/open loops, "Tiny Tales", reader-first tone), Settle's story-lesson-pitch structure and Dan Kennedy's direct-response approach not found in retrievable sources. Check the books directly.

## 4. Newsletters (curated, editorial, story-based)

### Takeaway
Morning Brew-style newsletters use a consistent modular layout with a casual voice; Milk Road explicitly cloned that model for crypto. Nothing retrievable on Lenny's or James Clear.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew: greeting and short welcome, headline news, market updates, sections broken into bullets, sponsored content, recap and recommendations; Mondays break down the economic calendar. Layout details come from 2019 and secondary reviews and may be outdated. — [Business Insider 2019 overview](https://www.businessinsider.nl/morning-brew-daily-business-newsletter-overview-2019-1); [Money Crashers](https://www.moneycrashers.com/?p=239416); [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/morning-brew-daily-newsletter)
- Milk Road: light-hearted easy reading on web3; content mix of meme of the day, audience questions, macro predictions, trend analysis, curated links; founders modeled it on Morning Brew ("Morning Brew of crypto"). Case study circa 2022. — [beehiiv case study](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/case-study-milk-road-ben-levy-shaan-puri)

### Inferences
- The shared pattern is a predictable ritual format plus a distinct voice; sponsorship slots are built into the layout.

### Gaps
- James Clear (3-2-1 Thursday), Lenny's (long-form editorial), Nicolas Cole, Chase Dimond: no sources retrieved. Open rates and length benchmarks missing.

## 5. Nurture / educational / drip and onboarding

### Takeaway
Val Geisler's "dinner party" approach delays selling: deliver the promised welcome value first, send from a real person, space emails 2-4 days.

### Cited Findings
- Dinner-party framework: 5-email series for ecommerce; first email does not sell and delivers the sign-up promise (often a coupon); welcome sent in real time from a real person; space emails 2-4 days; change the offer if the reader doesn't respond. — [Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-to-turn-new-subscribers-into-paying-customers-with-val-geislers-dinner-party-strategy/); [Privy, Sep 2022](https://www.privy.com/blog/val-geislers-dinner-party-strategy); [Forget the Funnel](https://forgetthefunnel.com/saas-email-marketing-for-humans)
- Joanna Wiebe/Copyhackers: favors PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution) for writing; advises not wholesale rewriting an existing control automation. No numbered sequence formula found. — [Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/10x-emails)

### Inferences
- Drip sequence skeleton: welcome (deliver promise) -> story/credibility -> education/quick win -> social proof -> soft offer.

### Gaps
- No length or open-rate benchmarks for nurture; Laura Belgray not found.

## 6. Event / webinar invitation and reminder sequences

### Takeaway
Use an invite 2-3 weeks out, reminders at 7 days, 1 day, 1 hour, with logistics in every message, plus replay and nurture follow-up. Lift percentages are vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
- Average show-up 40-50% (another source says 55%); a three-email reminder sequence reportedly lifts show-up from 35-40% to 55-65% (vendor claim). — [WebinarNinja](https://webinarninja.com/blog/webinar-email-sequence/); [Knak](https://knak.com/blog/the-different-types-of-webinar-emails/)
- Every reminder needs join link, date/time and time zone; calendar invite beats email alone. — [ClickMeeting](https://blog.clickmeeting.com/?p=14754)
- Replay within 1-3 hours; replay plus 2-3 nurture emails may double conversion vs replay alone (vendor claim). — [ON24](https://www.on24.com/blog/the-best-webinar-email-templates-to-boost-attendance/); [WebinarNinja](https://webinarninja.com/blog/reduce-webinar-no-show-rates/)

### Inferences
- One CTA per email: register, then add-to-calendar/join, then watch replay, then buy.

### Gaps
- Dates of these vendor posts not confirmed; no independent study.

## 7. Fundraising / nonprofit appeals

### Takeaway
Volume and year-end timing drive results; appeal-writing structure was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- M+R Benchmarks (2025 data, labelled 2026 edition): $54 raised per 1,000 fundraising messages (+4% vs 2024); $2.40 email revenue per subscriber (vs $1.87); nonprofits sent about 50 emails per subscriber, 31 of them fundraising; sample 100,000-recipient send yielded about 50 gifts and $5,400. — [M+R email benchmarks](https://mrbenchmarks.com/email)
- Nonprofits raise 17-34% of online revenue in December depending on cause. — [Climate Advocacy Lab on M+R 2025](https://www.climateadvocacylab.org/resource/2025-mr-benchmarks-study)
- Bloomerang (secondary): 31% of donations land on December 31; peak giving noon-7 pm. Clairification page contains an inserted nonsensical M+R attribution, so treat it as unreliable. — [Bloomerang](https://bloomerang.com/blog/the-final-countdown-5-ways-to-boost-year-end-donations)

### Gaps
- Appeal anatomy (story, ask, P.S., deadline), length, tone not sourced.

## 8. Plain text vs designed HTML; B2B vs B2C

### Takeaway
Plain text tends to win for B2B and reply-driven emails; B2C/ecommerce evidence favors HTML but is weak. No large independent study exists.

### Cited Findings
- B2B: plain text beat HTML roughly 4-9x when asking for a reply (practitioner claim, Databox); a HubSpot-referenced average has plain-text CTOR about 21% higher; Kalungi reports better deliverability and opens in nurture tests. — [Databox](https://databox.com/html-vs-plain-text-emails); [Stripo](https://stripo.email/blog/plain-text-vs-html-email-statistics-benchmarks-and-what-the-data-shows/); [Kalungi](https://www.kalungi.com/blog/plain-text-emails-inbox-deliverability)
- B2C: HTML recommended for retail-style sectors; Stripo notes stats mostly come from SaaS and tool vendors with no vertical split. People often prefer HTML even when plain text performs better. — [Unlayer](https://unlayer.com/blog/html-vs-plain-text-email); [Campaign Creators](https://www.campaigncreators.com/blog/html-vs-plain-text-emails)
